Buyer's Guide

Everything you need to know before buying your next video game.

💰

What is $ per Hour?

Our value metric divides the game's price by its average playtime. A 60-hour RPG at $60 is $1/hour; a 15-hour action game at the same price is $4/hour. Lower is better value.

🏆

Base vs Deluxe Editions

Deluxe editions rarely justify the premium. Most bundled DLC can be bought later on sale for a fraction of the cost. Stick to base editions unless you know you want a specific add-on.

📈

When to Wait for a Sale

Steam's summer and winter sales cut most catalog titles 50-75% off. AAA releases typically hit their first major discount 6 months after launch. Patience pays.

💻

Picking a Storefront

Steam has the biggest catalog and best refunds. GOG guarantees DRM-free downloads. Epic regularly offers free AAA games. Console stores price-match less but carry exclusives.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate are the playtime estimates?

Our average playtimes are drawn from community completion stats (main story + meaningful side content). Actual playtime varies widely — completionists often double these numbers, while players who rush campaigns may finish in 60–70% of the listed hours.

Why is $/hour the best metric for game value?

It captures the simplest, most durable signal: how much entertainment a dollar buys. A cheap 100-hour game can deliver more value than a pricey 15-hour one, even if the shorter game has higher production values. Combine this with Metacritic for quality.

Should I buy at launch or wait?

For single-player: wait. You'll get patched bugs, all DLC bundled, and a steep discount. For competitive multiplayer: buy early — player counts drop fast and matchmaking suffers. Live-service games with frequent seasons also favor early adoption.

What about free-to-play games?

We don't rank free-to-play titles since the $/hour metric breaks down at zero. Games like Fortnite, League of Legends, and Warframe are excellent choices but monetize through cosmetics and battle passes rather than an upfront price.

Which storefront has the best prices?

Fanatical, GreenManGaming, and Humble regularly beat Steam's own prices on the same Steam keys. For PlayStation and Xbox, first-party stores typically match or beat retail — Amazon used to lead but rarely has deep physical discounts anymore.
